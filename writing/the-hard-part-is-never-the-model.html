The hard part is never the model

What we learned building AI for Malaysian students. The model is the easy bit.

Akmal Akhpah·3 min read

People often ask which model we use at Pandai. It is a fair question, and the honest answer is that it matters less than you would think.

When we started building PBot, our AI study buddy, we assumed the difficult part would be the AI. It was not. A good model can explain photosynthesis beautifully. The trouble starts when a Form 4 student in Kelantan asks about photosynthesis the way their teacher taught it, in Bahasa Melayu, using the terms that will appear in their SPM paper.

That is where the real work is. Three things, mostly.

1. Staying inside the syllabus#

A student preparing for a national exam does not need the best answer on the internet. They need the answer that matches what their teacher taught and what the examiner expects.

So a lot of our effort goes into grounding. Mapping content to the national curriculum, topic by topic. Teaching PBot to stay inside it. Knowing when to say “this is beyond what you need for now” instead of showing off.

A tutor that is clever but off-syllabus can quietly do harm. It confuses the student and undermines the teacher.

2. Being correct in two languages#

Malaysian students learn in Bahasa Melayu and English, often switching between the two in the same sentence. Many models are much stronger in English. Science terms in Bahasa Melayu are specific, and a slightly wrong translation can cost marks.

We spend a surprising amount of time checking that answers are correct in both languages, not just fluent. Fluent and wrong is the most dangerous combination.

3. Being cheap enough for every student#

A tool that costs a few ringgit per student per day is fine for a pilot. It is not fine for two million students.

If we want AI help to reach a student in a rural school as easily as one in Kuala Lumpur, it has to be cheap enough to run nationwide. That pushes us toward smaller prompts, smarter caching, and asking whether a question really needs a large model at all.

What it is worth#

It is easy to get lost in all this and forget why we do it. Two numbers keep me honest.

Among students who use PBot, 65% see their final exam results improve. And AI Grader gives each teacher back around 20 hours a week, time that goes back into teaching instead of marking.

Those numbers belong to a team of engineers, content specialists and teachers who checked thousands of answers by hand. None of it came from picking the right model.

The model is the engine. The syllabus, the languages, the cost and the trust of teachers are the road. Most of our work is building the road.
