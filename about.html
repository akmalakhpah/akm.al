Secondary school

Two friends from school#

Khairul, Suhaimi and I met as teenagers in secondary school. We went off to different universities in the US (Purdue for Khairul, MIT for Suhaimi, Rensselaer for me) and somehow kept ending up back in the same room, building something together.

That friendship is close to thirty years old now. Almost everything good in my working life has come out of it. Aidan, then Pandai.

Rensselaer, Intel, UTM

Learning to be an engineer#

I studied computer science at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute in New York, with a focus on artificial intelligence. Back then AI mostly meant search algorithms and a lot of patience.

My first real job was as a software engineer at Intel in Cyberjaya. Intel is where I first used OKRs, and where I learned that good technical work should be tied to an outcome someone actually cares about. Later I went back to school for a Masters in real-time software at Universiti Teknologi Malaysia.

2009 to 2015

Performance in Public Health#

From 2009 to 2015 I worked at the World Health Organization headquarters in Geneva. I helped build ePMDS, the system WHO uses to plan and track performance for its staff around the world.

I worked side by side with HR specialists from many countries. The biggest lesson was humbling. One system has to survive very different local realities. What works in one office can feel strange in another, and the software has to respect that.

I think about that lesson every time we build something that has to work for a school in Kuala Lumpur and a school in rural Sarawak.

Since 2015

Goals and teams#

I introduced OKRs to my own team in 2015 and never looked back. In 2020, together with international OKR coaches, we founded FlowyTeam, a tool that helps companies build high-performance teams with OKRs, KPIs and 360 reviews.

We also grew OKR Institute, which now runs certification and coaching for organisations in many countries, and we are building OpenOKR as an open-source platform any team can use. I lead the technology side, including an AI assistant that helps teams draft, review and check in on their goals.

Since 2020

Education#

We launched Pandai in 2020 to help Malaysian students practise, get feedback and do better in school. In 2021 Pandai became the first Malaysian edtech company accepted into Y Combinator.

Since then the team has grown it to more than two million learners. The Ministry of Education appointed Pandai as a partner for its AI Powered Classroom pilot, and we have been working with the Sarawak Economic Development Foundation to bring AI assisted learning to schools in Borneo.

Built on those years, P&AI shares our learning tools as open source, through PAI Bot and Open School Syllabus. Our FDE Training is a 12-week programme that takes fresh graduates and turns them into AI Forward Deployed Engineers.

Across OKR Institute, FlowyTeam, Pandai and Aidan, I work with an engineering team of around fifty people.

Since 2007

Building for government#

In 2007 I started Aidan Technologies with the hope of making it easier for citizens to deal with government. In 2019 our team launched BORANG, which we believe was the first Government-as-a-Platform in Southeast Asia. It is now used by many Malaysian government agencies to deliver services to the public.

Today Aidan helps agencies use AI assistants in their work, and runs training on AI and agentic tools for public sector teams.

A few things I believe#

  • Ask who you want to become before you ask what you want to achieve.

    I borrowed this from someone wiser, and I still fail at it often.

  • Build the boring layer.

    Data, content, teacher networks and relationships with government compound over time. Models come and go.

  • Motivation, not content, is the biggest barrier to learning.

    Most students already have access to more material than they can use.

  • The hard part is never the model.

    It is the syllabus, the language, the cost, and the trust of teachers.

Advising#

I serve as an academic advisor for the Bachelor of Computer Science programme at Universiti Putra Malaysia, which mostly means listening to lecturers who know far more than I do.

Recognition#

I am grateful that the work of our teams has been noticed. Pandai was selected for the HundrED Global Collection 2026 and for MIT Solve’s Octava Social Innovation Challenge. These belong to the people who built Pandai, the teachers who trusted it, and the students who kept showing up.
